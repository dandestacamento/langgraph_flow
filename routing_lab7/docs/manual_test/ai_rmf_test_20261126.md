What two outcomes does the AI RMF aim for when addressing AI impacts?
The AI RMF aims to minimize potential negative impacts of AI systems, such as threats to civil liberties and rights, while also providing opportunities to maximize positive impacts.

What are the four AI RMF Core functions?
The four core functions of the AI RMF are GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE.

Assuming a governance structure is in place, must the AI RMF functions be performed in a fixed order?
No, functions may be performed in any order across the AI lifecycle as deemed to add value by a user of the framework.

The GOVERN function is designed to be infused throughout the MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE functions.

Does the AI RMF prescribe an organization's risk tolerance?
The AI RMF does not prescribe risk tolerance. Risk tolerance refers to the organization’s or AI actor’s readiness to bear the risk in order to achieve its objectives.

What seven characteristics of trustworthy AI are listed in the framework?
The seven characteristics of trustworthy AI listed in the AI RMF framework are: valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful bias managed.

Sources: NIST.AI.100-1.pdf (DOC-9695f988; v3; pages 5, 7, 8, 14, 17, 24, 25, 44, 47; retrieved, not verified)

Which questions do transparency, explainability, and interpretability respectively help answer?
Transparency can answer the question of “what happened” in the system. Explainability can answer the question of “how” a decision was made in the system. Interpretability can answer the question of “why” a decision was made by the system and its meaning or context to the user.

What three major categories of AI bias does NIST identify?
NIST has identified three major categories of AI bias to be considered and managed: systemic, computational and statistical, and human-cognitive.

An AI RMF use-case profile is based on the requirements, risk tolerance, and resources of the Framework user.

What is the difference between an AI RMF Current Profile and a Target Profile?
An AI RMF Current Profile indicates how AI is currently being managed and the related risks in terms of current outcomes, while a Target Profile indicates the outcomes needed to achieve the desired or target AI risk management goals. Comparing Current and Target Profiles likely reveals gaps to be addressed to meet AI risk management objectives.

What should be employed when choosing trustworthiness metrics and their precise threshold values?
Human judgment should be employed when deciding on the specific metrics related to AI trustworthiness characteristics and the precise threshold values for those metrics.
